## Ready-made anchors (reusable across interviews)

### "Tell me about yourself." — memorize this
> "I am a biomedical professional with international laboratory, cytology, teaching, and
> leadership experience, currently serving as a Lead Phlebotomist in the U.S. healthcare
> system. I am now seeking to apply my laboratory and clinical background in a research,
> laboratory operations, or clinical coordination role where my hands-on patient care,
> specimen handling, training, and biomedical science experience can add value."

### Explaining foreign education (confidence script)
> "I earned my Bachelor's in Biomedicine, a specialization in Oncology Cytology, and a
> Master's in Biopathology. My credentials have been evaluated and verified through WES to
> U.S. equivalency, and I've since gained direct U.S. hospital laboratory experience. That
> combination lets me bring both scientific depth and practical, day-to-day lab skills."

### Explaining certification status (honest script)
> "I don't currently hold the U.S. ASCP/MT certification. What I bring is a WES-verified
> biomedical Master's, 11 years of laboratory leadership, and current hands-on U.S. hospital
> lab experience. I'm also ready to complete certifications like CITI/GCP on your timeline. My
> focus is on the operations, specimen-handling, quality, and training skills this role needs."

### Bilingual advantage (say it proudly)
> "I'm a native Portuguese speaker, fluent in English, and conversational in Spanish. In a
> diverse patient population that helps me build trust quickly, explain procedures clearly,
> and improve collection success and compliance."

### ESL calm-down tactics (for the candidate, not to say aloud)
- Breathe, then use the short answer version first; expand only if asked.
- It's fine to say: "That's a great question — let me take a moment."
- Anchor answers in STAR so you never have to improvise structure.
